How solar works

You do not need to be an engineer to understand this.

The sun already reaches your roof every day. Solar simply catches part of it and turns it into power your home can use. Here is the whole thing in six steps.

1 Sunlight2 Panels3 Inverter 4 Your home5 Battery6 The grid

A slowly rotating model of a house with solar panels on the roof, an inverter on the wall, and a power line to the grid. The part being described lights up as you read each step below.

1

Sunlight hits your roof

This happens every day, for free, whether you catch it or not. Across the year the Philippines gets roughly four and a half to five and a half hours of strong sun a day.

Sunlight

2

The panels turn that light into electricity

They make DC power. There are no moving parts and no noise. They simply sit on your roof and work while the sun is up.

DC power

3

The inverter changes it into power your home can use

Your appliances run on AC, so the inverter converts the DC from the panels into AC. It is the brain of the system, and it also shows you how much your roof is producing.

DC to AC

4

Your home uses it first

Your fridge, lights, WiFi and aircon run on it. What your family uses during the day comes from your own roof instead of being bought from the grid.

AC power

5

A battery can store the extra, if you need one

A battery keeps some power for later, including during a brownout. Not every home needs one, and it adds real cost, so we will tell you straight if yours does not.

Storage

6

You stay connected to the grid

At night and on dark days your home draws from the grid as usual. With net metering, extra daytime power can be sent back and credited on your bill.

Net metering

One thing we always say plainly: solar is not automatic backup during a brownout. A normal grid connected system switches off when the grid goes down. Only a hybrid or off grid system with a battery keeps your home running. We talk about this with you before you decide anything.

Answers

Frequently asked questions

Is solar really worth it for my home?

It depends on how much electricity you use. The bigger your monthly bill, the faster solar pays for itself, because you are replacing power you are already buying. Homes with small bills wait longer to break even. That is why we look at your real usage before recommending any system.

How much can my bill go down?

A grid connected system covers the power your home uses while the sun is up. How much of your bill that removes depends on when your family uses electricity, how big the system is, and how much usable roof you have. We give you a real number for your home after the site assessment rather than a percentage from a brochure.

What happens when it rains?

The panels keep working, just at lower output. Clouds and the rainy season reduce production, they do not switch it off. A system is sized around the yearly average of about four and a half to five and a half hours of strong sun a day, not around perfect weather.

What happens during a brownout?

This is the most important answer, so we will say it plainly. A normal grid connected system switches itself off during a brownout. That is a safety rule, not a fault. It stops your power from flowing into lines that repair crews may be working on.

Only a hybrid or off grid system with a battery keeps your home running while the grid is down. If staying powered during brownouts matters to you, tell us early, because it changes the system we design.

Do I need a battery?

Not if you only want a lower bill. A grid connected system reduces your bill without one. You need a battery for two reasons: you want power during brownouts, or your family uses most of its electricity at night. Batteries add real cost, so we only recommend one when your answers call for it.

Can solar run my aircon?

Yes, if the system is sized for it. Aircon is usually the biggest load in a Filipino home, and it runs in the afternoon when the sun is strongest, which works in your favour. We count your aircon and everything else during the assessment so the system matches how your home really runs.

What happens to extra electricity?

Under net metering, a home system can send surplus daytime power back to the grid and receive credits on your bill. The application goes through your utility, and we guide you through the paperwork.

One honest detail: the credit is based on the utility's generation rate, which is lower than the full retail rate you pay. So exporting is a bonus, not the main saving. The main saving comes from using your own power directly.

How long does installation take?

The installation itself is usually short once the design is final. The overall schedule depends on your roof, the size of the system, and your utility's process. We put the dates in writing before we start, and we tell you straight away if anything moves.

What maintenance does it need?

Very little. Panels have no moving parts. They need occasional cleaning, especially after a long dry spell or if there is ash or dust in the air, and the inverter should be checked from time to time. We tell you exactly what your equipment needs and what we handle for you.

What warranty and support do I get?

The warranty comes from the specific panels and inverter in your design, so we put the terms in writing with your quotation instead of promising a single number here. After sales support is not an add on for us. It is the fourth of our four steps.
